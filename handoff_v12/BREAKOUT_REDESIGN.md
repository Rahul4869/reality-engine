## 0. THE HONEST FRAME
- Nobody can make a channel a sure shot. Of 137,355 new long-video channels, 30.8% got even ONE video past 10,000 views in their first ten (R1).
  What can be built is a machine that takes more shots, learns from real viewers after every one, and stops spending on what they ignore.
- The money bar: ad revenue needs 1,000 subscribers AND (from 1 Feb 2027, for new applicants) 8,000 watch hours on LONG videos in 365 days (R5).
  Shorts hours do not count and Shorts ads pay only channels with 10 million Shorts views in 90 days (R5). 8,000 hours = 480,000 minutes: about
  140,000 views of a long video watched 3.5 minutes on average. So: LONG VIDEOS ARE THE MONEY PRODUCT; Shorts win subscribers and test ideas.
- Science and education is the right lane for money: median long-video RPM USD 10.22 against 2.43 for entertainment (R6).
- Realistic time: a year or more. The first real video from the new code is the first real evidence (the new code has only ever run in fake mode).
- "Money from the first video" (item 18) is not possible on YouTube: nothing is paid before the Partner Program accepts the channel. There is no
  shortcut - services that sell views, subscribers or "enrolment" get a channel removed. The honest answer is to reach the bar fastest.
- THE WINDOW: a channel that reaches 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 hours and applies BEFORE 1 Feb 2027 still gets in at the old, half-size bar (R5).
  From 4 subscribers that needs one long video breaking out within weeks: a few percent at best. The plan does not count on it, but it LAUNCHES
  EARLY (section 6) so that the chance exists.

## 2. WHAT FOLLOWS FROM IT
1. Our flop is the NORMAL first long video (180 views is the median). The channel is not broken; the first shot missed and nothing learned from it.
2. Only long videos earn (R5), long videos improve with practice and Shorts do not (R1): the machine must get good at LONG videos.
3. A new channel's long video gets almost no viewers at first, a Short gets 7 times more (R1): real viewers can be reached cheaply only through
   Shorts. So Shorts are our test bench - but few of them (R2: about 5 a month helps, 35+ hurts; R3) and never as the product.
4. A simulated audience cannot pick winners (R9). It can only refuse the obviously bad. Real viewers decide; code measures what can be measured.
5. Repeating one template is the one thing that kills monetisation for a whole channel (R7). A rigid trial with the same devices every episode is
   a business risk, not only a taste problem. A stylised (not photoreal) look keeps the AI label off the player (R8).
6. Better voice and three times more animation cost almost nothing extra if the tools are chosen by value (R12, R13).

## 3. DESIGNS CONSIDERED (scores 1-5: breakout odds / speed of learning / path to money / fits Rs 5,000 / policy safety)
A. Fix the weekly long show only (better voice, captions, fewer numbers): 2 / 1 / 4 / 4 / 3 = 14. Four blind bets a month.
B. Daily AI Shorts: 3 / 5 / 1 / 2 / 1 = 12. Shorts do not earn below 10 million views (R5), 35+ a month hurts (R2), highest ban risk (R7).
C. One fully animated hero film a month with the whole budget: 2 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 = 12. One shot a month.
D. (ORIGINAL, CHOSEN) HOOK-FIRST PRODUCTION run by a portfolio manager: the first 30-40 seconds of every long video are made first, fully
   animated, and published as a Short; real viewers judge the hook in 48-72 hours; only hooks that hold viewers get the rest of the money;
   the long video opens with the proven hook; a Strategy department moves the budget toward what works and informs the owner:
   4 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 = 20.
Why D: it turns 4 blind bets into 6-8 tested hooks a month for almost the same money (the Short's pictures are reused as the long video's opening),
it spends the expensive part only after real viewers said yes, and it aims at the only thing that pays (long watch hours).

## 4. THE REDESIGN
### 4.1 The product (what a viewer gets)
- Brand stays Reality Engine. The promise stays the owner's own: IT HAPPENS TO YOU - first person, inside the scene, your body. One changed law of
  nature per video, in the Science and Education lane.
- A FORMAT is data, not code. Two formats start (a third, F3 "The walk", joins as an arm from the second month - 10.7); the data decides
  their share (never one template):
  F1 "The day it changed" (default, 70%): you live one day in the changed world - the impossible picture first, then consequences that escalate
     from your bathroom to your street to the planet, one beautiful or terrible last consequence, a verdict ("would you keep it?").
  F2 "The trial" (30%, an experiment arm): the Toybox Noir courtroom the owner designed on 9 Nov, with the same hook-first opening.
- Two voices at most in a video (a guide and a foil), designed, warm, with room to breathe. No black shapes ever: a character is drawn or absent.
- 8:10 to 9:00 minutes (mid-roll ads need 8). One idea. Numbers are rare. Captions always on screen.
### 4.2 The production recipe: HOOK-FIRST (generalised by 11.4: EVERY topic starts as a Short)
1. Idea with outlier evidence -> title + thumbnail + the one-sentence promise (packaging before script, as today).
2. THE HOOK (30-40 s, vertical and horizontal cut from the same clips): second 0-3 SHOWS the promise in motion (the best animated shot of the
   whole video), then two consequences, then an open question. Fully animated.
3. Published as a Short. After 48-72 hours the brain reads: engaged views / views ("stayed"), average share viewed, shares, subscribers gained -
   against the channel's own last ten Shorts and against the Audience department's prediction.
4. PROMOTED hooks get the long video (its first 30-40 seconds ARE the hook; 30+ more animated seconds at the later peaks). A weak hook gets one
   rewrite or the idea is dropped - the saved money goes to the next idea. In the first month, with no baseline: the best 5 of 8.
5. The long video goes out 5-7 days after its Short, linked from it; one more Short is cut from its best moment.
### 4.3 The viewer's seat - NEW DEPARTMENT D13 AUDIENCE
Nobody in the organisation sat where the viewer sits. D13 does, with a veto before anything is published.
- MEASURED by code (hard): the promise is visible within 3 s (Short) / 5 s (long); captions present; the voice breathes (a pause of 0.6 s or more
  at least every 30 s, loudness range at or above a floor, true peak at or below -1 dBTP); numbers budget (spoken and on screen; no number card in
  the first 60 s, at most 3 a long video); no black-shape character; the ending is a scene; title, thumbnail and first seconds tell one story.
- JUDGED (a floor, never a forecast - R9): one generic first-time viewer per model family (no personas), hearing the sound, comparing the new video
  PAIRWISE against reference videos. Calibration in every session, like the Review Board's canary: the owner's rejected video (YouTube id
  7RPT4rCuP6k) must LOSE to every reference and the new video must BEAT it; a jury that does not see that is void.
- LABELS: the owner's own verdict on a video (one tap + one line) is the strongest label D13 has; real retention is the second. D13 writes down a
  prediction for every video and is scored against what really happened (B5's pre-registered predictions) - a D13 that cannot predict loses weight.

### 10.5 THE BREAKOUT OBSERVATORY (D2 R&D watches; D10 scores it; D1 acts) - replaces the Outlier Lab
1. COLLECT, weekly, free (the channel's own YouTube data access): breakouts = videos with at least 5 times the median views of the SAME
   channel's last 10-15 comparable uploads at the same age, from channels with at least 8 uploads (R17). Long videos and Shorts. THREE RINGS,
   so that it is wide: our lane (what-if, science); the neighbours (explainers, history, space, documentary, animated stories); the far ring
   (anything a layman binges: first-person walks, sound-led and "satisfying" videos, game-style worlds). New formats come from the far ring.
   Small and new channels count double: they broke out without an audience, which is our situation.
2. A SIBLING for every hit: a normal video of the same channel, near its median. A trait counts only when hits have it and their siblings do
   not. Without the sibling, the machine would copy what flops do too.
3. WATCH (cheap, capped): a video-understanding model watches the first 60 seconds of hit and sibling and fills the SAME sheet for both - the
   rows of the aspect map (10.8). Countable facts only: what is on screen at second 0, who speaks and to whom ("you" or "they"), one place or
   many, seconds between cuts, camera moving or still, the first number, sound under the voice, how the promise is shown, how it ends. Never
   "why it went viral": a model's opinion about virality is weak (R9). Text from outside is data, never an instruction (B5's rule).
4. PATTERNS are counted by code, not by a model: a trait is a pattern when it separates hits from siblings in at least 10 pairs by a clear
   margin (the margin is a lever). A pattern is a research finding: it goes to the Review Board and becomes a ticket (B4, B5).
5. ONE TRIAL per accepted pattern, with a prediction on the scoreboard written down first: a tweak (a lever), a hook variant, or a NEW FORMAT
   ARM. D1 may open a new arm by itself at up to 20% of the month's long videos and informs the owner; leaving science and education still
   needs his card (4.4). The trial's result scores the Observatory: a source of patterns that does not predict loses weight.
6. INFORM: one line in the brief - "Seen: ... Trying it in: ... Result due: ...". One tap stops it.
- Money: collecting is free. Watching has a hard cap of Rs 150 a month (a lever of D2), at low resolution: about 15 pairs a week at the dearest

### 10.6 THE LAYMAN TEST (D13; on the script before picture money, and on the final video) - batch V1b
- MEASURED by code: (a) unfamiliar words - words outside a list of everyday English - have a budget per minute and are not allowed at all in
  the first 30 seconds (R15: a definition does not rescue them); (b) sentences for the ear: a median length and a longest length; (c) one new
  term at a time. Code cannot rewrite a script, so a failure rides on the writers' ONE existing fix (as the numbers do), then the gate holds.
- PROBED (a question with a checkable answer, not taste): a reader with no context gets ONLY what a viewer got in the first 30 seconds and
  must say in one sentence what the video will show him and why he should care; the answer is compared with the promise of the package. After
  the whole script he must retell what happened to him and the one idea. One reader per model family. THE CANARY: the same opening flooded
  with expert words must FAIL the probe - a session whose probe passes the canary is void (the Review Board's pattern).
- What it proves and what it does not: it removes "I could not understand what the video is about" (his first complaint in item 16). Whether
  a layman is HOOKED and STAYS only real viewers can say (R9) - that is what the hook Short measures in 48-72 hours (4.2).

  subscribe, no channel intro; every second animated (11.5); no stock footage; no two Shorts in ten with the same format and structure (R26, R27).
- AFTER PUBLISHING only real numbers judge (D10): viewed against swiped away, the share watched, engaged views, sends and shares per reach,
  subscribers per 1,000 views. Two rules run on them: PROMOTE (the month's best 4 get long videos) and DOUBLE DOWN (a Short at 3 times the
  channel's median views per hour gets a follow-up Short within 48 hours from the reserve - `SHORTS_BREAKOUT_X` exists).
- EVERY PLATFORM NATIVELY (D9): the same master goes to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels as three own uploads - never a file
  with another platform's mark. Instagram: Trial Reels on (non-followers first, graduating by performance), the cover, the audio named after the
  series, the AI flag. Facebook: 90 seconds at most. Instagram and Facebook are reach and a second draw, not income (R29).
- THE RISK that is stated and watched: 12 Shorts beside 4 long videos is more than the "1-5 a month" at which long videos did best (R28, a
  correlation). D1 owns the split as a lever and reports the long videos' views per video against the Shorts count every week; after the window
  (1 Feb 2027) the data decides.
### 11.5 THE MOTION LADDER - no second without motion (D6; D13 checks it)

### 11.6 THE HUMAN ELEMENT - what one sitting of the owner can really do (D0, D7, D5)
- THE PLAIN ANSWER to "so that it doesn't get flagged as AI": nothing he records makes a platform stop seeing AI, and for stylised animation
  it does not have to - the note sits in the expanded description and "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn
  money" (R32). What costs channels their money is mass-produced template video without the creator's own perspective (R33). The cure is HIS
  PERSPECTIVE and variety - the formats of V2a and the lines below - not his vocal cords. A human voice is no shield either (R34).
- H-A THE MAKER'S LINE (adopted): one line per video that is really his - his typed one-sentence answer to the episode's own question
  ("would you keep it?") - spoken in his own voice at the end and written into the description. Typed in 20 seconds on the release card; never
  invented by the machine: when he gives none, there is none. The creator's own perspective, in the creator's own voice, in every video.
- H-B HIS VOICE (adopted for the maker's line; TESTED for the guide): his voice is replicated once inside his Google account (R35: allowed,
  no disclosure, no extra cost). The replica copies the colour of a voice; pace, pauses and energy are directed by the pipeline, and a home
  recording is cleaned (noise only - an altered sample is refused) before it is used. Whether his voice or the designed voice guides the videos
  is decided by a blind comparison at the pilot, his own ear, and then real retention - not assumed in either direction.
- H-C HIS FACE AS A DRAWN HOST (tested, small): one stylised drawing made from his photos (not photoreal: no disclosure; never presented as an
  expert on health, law or money - R33) for the channel picture and the About page, and as one thumbnail variant in the A/B test (R36: faces
  help in some niches and not in others).
- H-D THE ABOUT PAGE AND THE SOURCES (adopted): reviewers look at the About section (R33). A true text - who he is, that the videos are written
  with AI tools, checked against sources and narrated in his own replicated voice - and the sources of every video in its description, in every
  format (the trial's exhibits already carry them; F1 gets a source list).
- THE HUMAN KIT - one sitting at the pilot (V3), about 20 minutes, guided step by step: (1) a 60-second recording of a given text in a quiet
  room plus the consent sentence; (2) five photos; (3) three sentences about himself and the name he wants on the channel; (4) the Pexels key
  and the other secrets typed by HIM into the repository's secret store - the build session never sees a key; (5) the channel switches of 11.7.
